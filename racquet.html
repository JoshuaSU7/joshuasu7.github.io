Heated Racquet Grip

A modular electric grip-heating system that keeps your hands warm during outdoor winter tennis — and fits any modern racquet without modifying the frame.

PCB Design KiCAD Power Management Product Design

The Problem

As an avid tennis player, one of the biggest obstacles for recreational players is playing outdoors in winter. Cold hands mean you lose your feel for the ball, and the racquet slips constantly.

None of the existing options work. Gloves make it impossible to grip the racquet tightly and correctly. Disposable hand warmers only help between points and lose their heat quickly. You could design an entirely new handle with integrated heating, except the racquet market is dominated by a few companies who make frame and handle customization deliberately difficult and labour-intensive.

The Build

I built a first-of-its-kind modular electric racquet grip heating system that delivers continuous heat to the user’s hands. It works with any modern tennis racquet from the major brands, and the same approach could extend to other sports. Along the way I picked up basic circuit analysis, PCB design and power management.

Most of the design was constrained by physical space — there is very little room inside a grip for batteries, a PCB and a heating element. I used KiCAD to design a board connecting the heating unit to the batteries, a switch and a charging circuit, had it manufactured through JLCPCB, and validated it myself.

Exact design choices and specifications for the heating unit are private for now. This page will be updated once the design is finalised or released as a product.

Next Steps

Building a casing for the PCB, further improving space efficiency, and getting feedback from other recreational players and product managers.
