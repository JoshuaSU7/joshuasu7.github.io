Where I’m Headed

And why it matters to me.

The Big Picture

My goal is to use my foundation in electrical engineering to design hardware and systems that directly improve people’s quality of life. The projects I’m drawn to such as radar and RF links, crash detection wearables, human-computer interfaces for paralyzed individuals, all share a thread: engineering in service of safety, accessibility, or precision.

I’m most interested in work that bridges low-level hardware design such as RF and antennas, analog circuits, PCB design, embedded systems, signal processing, with applications where getting it right actually matters: sensing and detection systems, medical devices, assistive technology, aerospace.

Focus Areas

RF & Antenna Systems

Radar, phased arrays and communication links; designing the front end that decides what a system can actually see or reach.

Hardware & PCB Design

Turning a working schematic into a board that survives real conditions: power integrity, signal integrity, layout discipline.

Sensing & Safety Systems

Embedded sensing and signal processing for proactive safety devices, from crash detection to fall prevention.

Assistive Technology

Human-computer interfaces and control systems that restore independence to people with physical disabilities.

My Path Forward

Near term — Undergrad

Build a strong technical foundation

Finish my ECE degree at Georgia Tech focused on signal processing and sensing, alongside computer architecture and automation coursework. Keep pushing the radar board through to fabrication and measurement, take on leadership in the teams I’m part of, and pursue hardware or firmware internships for industry experience.

Mid term — Early career

Work on hardware that ships

Join a company building RF systems, aerospace electronics, wearable medical devices or safety hardware. Contribute across the full design cycle; circuit design, prototyping, testing, production, and work alongside engineers from other disciplines to get exposure to as many industries as I can.

Long term

Lead and innovate

Move into a senior engineering or research role driving product direction, and potentially pursue a graduate degree if it opens doors to deeper research in RF, embedded systems or signal processing.

What Drives Me

Impact over complexity

The best design is the one that solves the actual problem, not the most intricate one.

Rigor in design

Simulate it, justify every number, then measure it and find out where you were wrong.

Accessibility & inclusion

Technology should widen who gets to participate, not narrow it.

Collaborative problem-solving

Hardware is a team sport.

Let’s connect

If you’re working on something at the intersection of hardware and human impact, I’d love to hear about it.
