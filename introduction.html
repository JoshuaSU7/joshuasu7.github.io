About

Hi, I’m Joshua

I’m an electrical engineering student at Georgia Tech working on RF and antenna design, PCB design, and embedded systems — hardware that has to survive contact with the real world.

Most of what I build sits somewhere between signals and physical hardware: a phased array that steers a 10.5 GHz beam electronically, a ground station that has to hold a link to a rocket, a wearable that has to decide in milliseconds whether someone just crashed. Outside of school I cook, play tennis and table tennis, and play classical piano.

How I got here

My interest in signal processing started in my senior year of high school, doing research in the Kamaleswaran lab at Emory University (now Duke), where I investigated machine learning models for early classification of interstitial lung disease.

I started at Georgia Tech in biomedical engineering, joined the Medical Robotics Club, and began the electrooculography eye-tracking project. That project in particular made it clear that electrical engineering was the better fit, so I switched majors starting Spring 2026.

Once I understood the real scope of the major, I got a lot more willing to work outside bioengineering. I joined the SiliconJackets analog mixed-signal team doing transistor-level ASIC design, then moved into RF and antenna work with GT Experimental Rocketry’s ground systems team and PCB design with the Yellow Jacket Space Program’s avionics group. The phased array radar board grew out of that — it is the project that has taught me the most.

I’m looking forward to finishing what I’ve started and trying projects in industries I haven’t touched yet — autonomous robots and vehicles, aviation, and space.
